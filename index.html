01 — Threat Detection

Phishing Attacks

Phishing is a social engineering attack where criminals impersonate trusted entities to steal your credentials, data, or money.

Threat Frequency92%
Success Rate (no training)75%
Prevention with awareness88%

EXAMPLE — PHISHING EMAIL (hover flags)

⚠ SUSPICIOUS
Mail — Inbox
From: security@paypa1-alerts.net [!]
Subject: URGENT: Your account will be suspended [!]
To: valued.customer@email.com [!]

Dear Valued Customer,


We have detected unusual activity on your account. Your access will be terminated in 24 hours unless you verify immediately.


Click below to secure your account:


→ Verify Account Now ←

PayPal Security Team
No contact information provided

HOW TO SPOT PHISHING

🔍 Check the sender's domain

Attackers use lookalike domains: "paypa1.com" instead of "paypal.com". Always inspect the full email address carefully.

⚡ Beware urgency & threats

"Act now or your account will be deleted!" — Legitimate companies never pressure you with artificial deadlines.

🔗 Hover before you click

Hover over any link to preview the real URL in your browser's status bar before clicking. If it looks wrong, it is wrong.

📎 Never open unexpected attachments

Invoice.pdf.exe, resume.zip, or any unsolicited file is a vector for malware. When in doubt, don't open it.

📞 Verify through official channels

If you're unsure, contact the company directly via their official website or phone number — not the contact info in the suspicious email.

02 — Access Security

Password Security

Weak passwords are the single most common entry point for attackers. A strong password policy is your first line of defense.

PASSWORD STRENGTH ANALYSIS

✓ DO

  • Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password)
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)
  • Use unique passwords for every account
  • Use passphrases of 16+ characters
  • Change passwords after any breach
  • Mix uppercase, lowercase, numbers & symbols

✗ DON'T

  • Reuse passwords across multiple sites
  • Use personal info (birthdate, name, pet)
  • Share passwords via email or chat
  • Use keyboard patterns (qwerty, 12345)
  • Save passwords in plain text files
  • Use dictionary words as passwords

Use a VPN

A Virtual Private Network encrypts all your traffic, masking it from ISPs, hackers on the same network, and surveillance.

Essential on public WiFi
🔒

HTTPS everywhere

Always check for the padlock icon and "https://" in your browser. Never enter passwords or payment info on HTTP sites.

Check before submitting data
📡

Secure your router

Change the default admin password, use WPA3 encryption, disable WPS, and keep firmware updated.

Do this today
📵

Avoid public WiFi

Free airport, café, or hotel WiFi is an attacker's playground. Use your mobile hotspot or VPN instead.

High risk environment
🛡

Enable firewall

Your OS firewall monitors incoming and outgoing connections. Keep it enabled and configure alerts for unknown apps.

Always keep active
🔄

Update everything

Most exploits target known unpatched vulnerabilities. Enable auto-updates for your OS, browser, and all apps.

Weekly habit

You receive an email from "support@amaz0n-help.com" saying your order is on hold. What do you do?

✓ Correct answer: C

"amaz0n-help.com" is not Amazon's domain. Always navigate directly to the official website rather than clicking email links.

Which of these passwords is the strongest?

✓ Correct answer: C

At 21 characters with mixed case, numbers, and symbols — and no predictable patterns — this passphrase would take centuries to crack.

You're at a café and need to check your bank account. What's the safest approach?

✓ Correct answer: B

Public WiFi — even password-protected — can be monitored. Mobile data uses encrypted cellular networks and is far safer for sensitive transactions.
